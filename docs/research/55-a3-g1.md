## 1. The G-1 line, and what it means for A-3

```

     bars: agreement >= 0.90 (met); median |dDTW| < 1.0 pp (met)
```

**G-1 passes, and it passes by a distance nobody has to squint at.** Refit the
model with a week of season held out, read every game's defence-season effect
from the fit that never saw that week, and the adjudication barely moves: 1,136
of 1,139 games keep the same verdict bucket, and on the games the variant
touches at all the two arms' DTW% differ by a median of **0.05 percentage
points** against a bar of 1.0. The bars asked for 0.90 agreement and got 0.997;
they asked for under 1.0 pp and got a twentieth of it.

What that licenses, in document 52 §5's own words: *"On pass, production keeps
the in-sample read with this bound recorded."* Production therefore reads `u_d`
from the single in-sample fit, and the bound recorded beside it is this
document's first line. The nineteen fold traces are diagnostic, not production
artifacts.

6's gates are answered — G-1 here, G-2 and G-3 in document 53 — and what remains

### 1a. What the three disagreements are

Three games out of 1,139 change bucket between the arms, and all three make the
**same** move: `scoreboard holds -> too close to call`.

| Game | actual | in-sample → week-out DTW% | ΔDTW | deserved margin | events |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `2025_14_CIN_BUF` | +5 | 60.1 → 57.7 | 2.45 pp | +0.59 → +0.36 | 2 |
| `2025_11_CHI_MIN` | −2 | 39.3 → 40.5 | 1.21 pp | −1.09 → −0.96 | 2 |
| `2023_06_MIN_CHI` | −6 | 40.0 → 41.0 | 1.02 pp | −1.40 → −1.23 | 3 |

Every one of them is a game already sitting within 1.2 points of the
`too close to call` band's edge — one at the 0.60 edge, two at the 0.40 edge —
nudged across it by a defence-season effect estimated without its own week. Not
one crosses to or from `clear flip`, and not one has a deserved margin further
than 1.4 points from a tie. That is the shape a reader should want: if the
week-out read were materially different, the disagreements would be scattered
across the other five transitions, and there are none.

### 2a. The postseason fold (F-2)

The nineteenth fold holds out weeks 19-22 **together** — 147 worthy throws, 2,822
rows fitted, seed `20260827 + 19 = 20260846`. This closes the gap document 53 §6
disclosed: round 5's eighteen folds left every postseason game's week inside
every fold's training data, so G-1 was measured on the 1,091 games weeks 1-18
covered and the ~48 postseason games were excluded as the conservative choice.
G-1 is now measured on **all 1,139 games**, with no exclusion to defend.

Two guards make that literal rather than intended: `research/69` refuses to start
unless the fold list partitions the weeks the frame actually carries (no week
covered twice, no week uncovered), and `68.variant_pass` refuses if any game is
scored by more than one fold.

## 3. F-3 — round 4's audit, reproduced at the new spec

Document 54's F-3 requires the in-sample arm to be re-fitted at F-1's spec and
round 4's audit re-run on it *before* G-1 is computed, so the gate compares like
with like. Two of the four numbers do not depend on the fit at all and are held
exactly; the two that do get F-3's tolerances.

```
  games                      round 4     1139   now     1139   ok   (exact — data, not the fit)
  affected_games             round 4     1033   now     1033   ok   (exact — data, not the fit)
  bucket_moves               round 4      137   now      136   ok   (document 54 F-3: +/- 5 games)
  median_abs_delta_dtw_pp    round 4     1.62   now     1.59   ok   (document 54 F-3: +/- 0.2 pp)
  mean interval width        round 4 v1.3 0.0383 / variant 0.0511
                             now          0.0383 / 0.0514   (reported only — F-3 names two statistics)
  F-3: PASS
```

**One game and 0.03 pp.** The variant's headline — a verdict bucket moved on
about 12% of 2022-2025 games — is not an artifact of a sampler setting: change
the number of draws by a factor of four and the count moves from 137 to 136.

A consequence worth stating plainly, because it looks like a rule being relaxed:
`research/67`'s round-4 reproduction tripwire (the default fit's `sigma_d` to
twelve decimal places) was written to prove that round 5's *refactor* changed no
number, at A-2's spec. F-1 changes the spec deliberately, so the tripwire is now
enforced **only** at A-2's spec and reported otherwise — the default fit's
`sigma_d` moves 0.2544 → 0.2575, which is sampler noise between two specs and
not a defect. F-3's audit reproduction is the check that carries the weight
instead, and it is a stricter one: it tests the numbers the product actually
shows.

## 4. The three named games, and the ten largest disagreements

### 4a. The named games under both arms

All three keep their bucket, and two of the three move by less than half a
percentage point.

| Game | actual | v1.3 DTW% | in-sample | week-out | bucket |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `2025_17_DET_MIN` | +13 | 54.8 | 37.5 | **37.2** | clear flip, both arms |
| `2025_13_DEN_WAS` | −1 | 14.5 | 29.3 | **29.5** | scoreboard holds, both arms |
| `2022_13_WAS_NYG` | 0 | 80.7 | 95.4 | **96.6** | clear flip, both arms |

The mechanism is visible in the ledger rows. In `2025_17_DET_MIN` the two
Detroit interceptions are priced at P(escape) 0.320 and 0.477 in-sample, 0.326
and 0.486 week-out — the defence-season effect barely notices the loss of one
week's throws, because a defence-season carries about 22 worthy throws and a
fold removes one or two of them.

`2022_13_WAS_NYG` moves the most of the three, 95.4 → 96.6, and it is the game
that should move most: eight dropped-pick rows, seven of them Washington throws
the 2022 NYG defence-season failed to intercept — the worst-rated defence-season
in the model, and the one V-8's ruling R-3 is about. Every one of those seven is
given a *lower* escape probability by the fit that never saw week 13, by 1.6 to
6.5 pp (0.598 → 0.533, 0.583 → 0.523, 0.514 → 0.453 are the three largest).
That is self-fulfilment made visible: the in-sample fit lets these drops lower
its own estimate of New York's hands and so charges less luck for them, while
the held-out fit rates that defence higher and charges more. It is worth **1.2
pp of DTW%** on the game constructed to make it worst.

## 5. What the week-out arm says about the 12%

Because the week-out arm exists, its own audit against v1.3 can be reported, and
it is the honest robustness check on document 50's headline:

| Arm | bucket moves | share | median \|ΔDTW\| affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| in-sample, re-fit at F-1 | 136 | 11.94% | 1.59 pp |
| **week-out** | **139** | **12.20%** | **1.65 pp** |
| round 4's in-sample, at A-2 | 137 | 12.03% | 1.62 pp |

The 12% is not an in-sample artifact. Score every game from a fit that never saw
its week and the count goes **up** by three, not down. Whatever else is arguable
about the dropped-pick variant — and document 52 §6 lists what is — "the model
is reading the game it is adjudicating" is not one of the arguments.

### 5a. A thing the folds said that is now worth more than it was

Document 53 §2d recorded `sigma_d`'s posterior mean running 0.205-0.294 across
round 5's eighteen folds, and warned that this "mixes real fold-to-fold
variation with the sampler noise the failures above are made of" — a caveat that
was correct when seven folds had not converged.

It can be retired. The nineteen folds at F-1's spec put `sigma_d` at 0.2075-0.2930,
and fold by fold across the two independent specs the values correlate at
**r = 0.988**, with a mean absolute gap of 0.0029 and a maximum of 0.0062. The
per-fold standard deviation is the same to three decimals (0.0222 at A-2's spec,
0.0220 at F-1's). **The fold-to-fold spread in the defence-season variance is the
data, not the sampler**: which week you hold out really does change how much
spread the model sees among defence-seasons, by about ±0.02 around 0.257, and
that reproduces across two sampler specs. It is still not a G-1 result, and it
is still descriptive — but it is now a reproducible descriptive fact rather than
a number contaminated by unconverged fits.
